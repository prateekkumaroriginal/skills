A guide for choosing animations and transitions.

## Animations

Animation controls how something behaves over time.
A circle expanding to show a growing radius is an animation.

Here's an example of deciding an appropriate animation. Here the `dot` is just a means to an end, nothing specific. A pulsing dot draws attention to a location, a moving dot shows the direction of travel, and the trail it leaves shows the route already taken.

## Transitions

A transition controls how the viewer gets from one shot, scene, or state to the next.
A circle expanding to uncover the next scene is doing the job of a transition.

Choose a transition by thinking about how the next scene relates to the one the viewer just saw. The `card` and `grid` are just a means to an end, nothing specific.

Imagine opening a card from a grid. Expanding the card in place shows where the detail came from. Sliding the grid aside makes the detail feel like the next page. Cutting straight to the detail gives the viewer the answer immediately. All three reach the same destination, but each tells the viewer something different about how they got there.

## Emphasis

Focus is what you want the viewer to notice at a given moment. Animation can draw attention to something inside a scene. A transition can carry that attention into the next scene.
