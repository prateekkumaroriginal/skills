Choreography is the art of arranging, moving, and transforming on-screen elements so each moment feels right. Animation, transitions, and focus are central to it. They shape how elements move, how one scene flows into the next, and where the viewer’s attention goes. Let the moment set the pace and intensity. A small change might call for a gentle shift, a surprise for a sharp cut, and a detail worth reading for a moment of stillness.

## Animations

Animation controls how something behaves over time.
A circle expanding to show a growing radius is an animation.

Here's an example of deciding an appropriate animation. Here the `dot` is just a means to an end, nothing specific. A pulsing dot draws attention to a location, a moving dot shows the direction of travel, and the trail it leaves shows the route already taken.

## Transitions

A transition controls how the viewer gets from one shot, scene, or state to the next.
A circle expanding to uncover the next scene is doing the job of a transition.

Choose a transition by thinking about how the next scene relates to the one the viewer just saw. The `card` and `grid` are just a means to an end, nothing specific.

Imagine opening a card from a grid. Expanding the card in place shows where the detail came from. Sliding the grid aside makes the detail feel like the next page. Cutting straight to the detail gives the viewer the answer immediately. All three reach the same destination, but each tells the viewer something different about how they got there.

## Focus/emphasis

Focus is what you want the viewer to notice at a given moment. Animation can draw attention to something inside a scene. A transition can carry that attention into the next scene. Ponder the question "Where on screen should the viewer be looking right now?"

The following examples are just a means to an end, nothing specific.

- **Zoom into a detail.** Show a full calendar. As the narrator says "these two days overlap," zoom into the two entries and stop with both dates readable. The viewer sees where to look and why those entries belong together.
- **Reveal content progressively.** Show a chart up to the day of a policy change. As the narrator explains what happened afterward, reveal the rest of the line from left to right. Attention follows the newly exposed edge, while the earlier trend stays visible for comparison.
- **Build from a blank screen.** Start empty. On "a request arrives," one request appears. On "it waits," a queue forms around it. On "a worker picks it up," the worker appears and the request moves toward it. The viewer learns where to look as each part becomes relevant.
